Three ways back into what you wrote

The entry is the unit. Everything else — the timeline, the calendar, the attachments — is a way of finding one again.

Timeline

Entries grouped by year and month, newest first, each with a thumbnail of the first photograph or video in it.

Calendar

A month grid where the days you wrote something are filled in, so a gap in a month is visible at a glance.

The day editor

One page per day: a title, the words, and the attachment tiles beside them.

Photographs and video

Taken with the camera or picked from the phone, then copied into that day's folder.

Sound

A voice memo recorded inside the entry, from the phone's own microphone, saved with the day it belongs to.

Any file

A letter as a PDF, a document, a scan — attached and openable from the entry without leaving the journal.

Your entries are files, not a subscription

A journal kept in a format only one app can open is a journal with an expiry date. This one writes plain files.

A folder per day. Every entry is a small folder named for its date, holding a readable entry.json and the photographs, recordings and files that went with it — all in the app's own storage on the phone.

Nothing to export from. No database to migrate and no service to keep paying. What the app writes is what a person could open on a computer.

It updates itself. The screen you use is a page the app holds, so a release can replace it without an installer and a fix arrives the next time you open the journal. The update check asks for one file and carries nothing from your entries.

Private by construction

No account, no analytics, no advertising, no third-party libraries watching what you write.

Entries, photographs, recordings and files stay in the app's storage on the phone. They are not uploaded, synced or backed up by us, because there is no server of ours to hold them.

The camera and the microphone are used only while you are taking a picture or recording a memo, and stop when you stop. The timeline, the calendar and the editor all work with the phone in flight mode.

The full statement is in the privacy policy.
